Nvidia's chip works differently. Picture ten thousand kindergarteners holding crayons. None of them knows calculus… Some eat the paste. But they ALL color at the exact same moment. In tech speak, that's a GPU.

A factory for tiny math, done all at once. Then AI researchers notice a secret.

What is AI training? TRILLIONS of tiny math problems. Exactly what those kindergarteners do best.

> **GPU:** I used to draw dragons for video games.

> **Investor:** Great. I'll take ten thousand of them!

Nvidia isn't just selling shovels in a gold rush… It's selling the ONLY shovels on earth. Why can't buyers just switch brands? Because AI engineers write their code for Nvidia's own tools. And Beijing needs those shovels too.

Why does China care so much about a gaming chip? Because whoever owns the fastest chips trains the smartest AI. And Washington worries smart AI can also guide… modern weapons.

> **Hawk:** That chip is a missile brain!

## 3. The Locked Door  ·  3:41

`Thẻ chương (chỉ hiện chữ, không đọc): The Paper Wall.`

*October 7, 2022. Washington, D.C.*

Washington builds a wall across the Pacific… It's a wall made of paperwork. In October 2022, export controls block top AI chips from going to China. The rule works like a highway speed limit. Is your chip too fast?

You need a federal license. Shipping to Beijing? Good luck getting that stamp. So how does Nvidia respond? Engineers design slightly slower chips — just for China. Problem solved?

**Not for long.**

In October 2023, Washington lowers the speed limit again. Those China-only chips get banned too. So Nvidia designs an even SLOWER chip: the H20. Think of a Ferrari… with a lawnmower engine.

> **GPU:** Why am I so slow?  
> **GPU 2:** You're the LEGAL model.  

Then, in April 2025, Washington restricts the H20 as well. Nvidia takes a 5.5-billion-dollar charge on unsold inventory. They baked the cake, and Uncle Sam cancelled the party.

> **Official:** This chip is too fast for China.  
> **Salesman:** No problem! We made a slower one.  
> **Official:** That one is also too fast.  
> **Salesman:** So we made an even slower one!  
> **Official:** Still… too fast.  
> **Salesman:** What if I just ship the empty box?  
> **Official:** Fill out form seven.  

Every physical door to China looks locked tight. Why? Because the rules watch where physical boxes go… **Not where the math goes.**


## 4. The Way Around  ·  5:05

`Thẻ chương (chỉ hiện chữ, không đọc): The Long Cable.`

*2024. Southeast Asia.*

Border guards are GREAT at stopping cardboard boxes. They are terrible at stopping Wi-Fi. Imagine your mom bans a PlayStation from your bedroom. How do you beat the ban? Your neighbor buys ten PlayStations instead.

He keeps every console inside his OWN house. Then he runs a long controller cable through your window. You don't move the machine… **You just move the game.**

In Silicon Valley, that's called cloud computing. Shipping an H100 chip to China requires a US license. Putting it in a data center in Malaysia? Generally… legal. Letting overseas clients rent that power over the internet? Also legal.

> **Official:** Did any of these chips go to China?  
> **Tech:** Nope. The servers stay riiight here in Malaysia.  
> **Official:** Then what leaves the building?  
> **Tech:** Just the login… and the answers.  
> **Official:** Is there a form for answers?  
> **Tech:** You tell me, man.  
> **Official:** …Fill out form seven.  

Lawmakers have a name for this gap. **The cloud loophole.**

Remember the neighbor…

Because in Singapore, a tiny firm is about to test that cable.

Everyone wins… Especially Nvidia's accountants. Next, Megaspeed sets up a Malaysian arm called Speedmatrix. Why Malaysia? Cheap land, big power grids — and a short cable from Singapore.

Washington hawks say Megaspeed proves the export wall needs cloud rules. Nvidia's camp says cutting off buyers only forces China to build rival chips faster. Megaspeed insists it ran a lawful commercial cloud under US rules.

So how did 7.5 billion dollars of chips end up in this story? As far as anyone has shown… they DIDN'T sneak into China in dark trucks. They went to Southeast Asia, where renting them out was generally allowed.

So did the wall leak?

**Maybe… but not the way you picture it.**

Reports say Chinese companies rented that computing power from afar. Bloomberg found no proof the chips themselves crossed over. **The wall held. The cable just went over it.**
